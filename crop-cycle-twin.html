Planned harmonization

Resample every layer to one monthly grid (about 1 km), average satellite pixels over the field, then pair each with ground records such as rain gauges or soil surveys to correct bias before scoring rotations.

Plain-language guide

Evapotranspiration

Water lost from soil and plants to the air. High values mean crops need more water.

Water balance

Rain minus evapotranspiration. Below zero, soil dries unless you irrigate.

NDVI

A satellite greenness score. Falling NDVI during the season signals stress; near-zero means bare soil.

Soil moisture anomaly

How wet or dry the soil is compared with normal for that time of year.

Frost risk

Cold months where young crops can be damaged. Here, months averaging under 5 °C are flagged.

Satellite vs ground data

Satellites cover everywhere but see averages over large pixels. Gauges and soil samples are exact at one spot. Using both is more accurate.

Climate comes from a built-in NASA POWER monthly climatology (T2M, PRECTOTCORR, ALLSKY_SFC_SW_DWN). The live button fetches fresh values when this file runs on your own computer. Crop and soil numbers are simplified agronomic estimates for planning, not field-tested forecasts.
